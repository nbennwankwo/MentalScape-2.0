# Interview guide for planners and commissioners

## Purpose

To check whether the questions in [01_decision_makers.md](../01_decision_makers.md) are the questions real planners ask, and to learn how they make allocation decisions today. The aim is to learn, not to pitch MentalScape.

## Who to speak to

Three to five people from this list is enough to start.

- ICB mental health commissioners or commissioning managers
- Trust service, operations or performance managers in mental health
- NHS or local authority analysts who produce reports for these people
- Public health or health inequalities leads
- Workforce planners

LinkedIn, Loughborough alumni, NHS analyst communities and NHS England's public events are good places to find them.

## Outreach message

> Hello [name], I'm a recent MSc Data Science graduate building an open source decision support tool for NHS mental health service planning, using published NHS England data. Before I build further I want to understand how planning decisions are really made. Would you be open to a 20 minute call? I'm not selling anything and I won't quote you by name. Thank you, Kemi

## Before the call

- Ask permission to take notes, and say notes will be kept without names
- Have no slides and no demo. Showing the product too early turns an interview into a sales call

## Questions

Questions ask about what people have already done, not what they might do. Follow up any answer with "tell me more about that" or "what happened next".

**Their role**

1. Talk me through your role and the decisions you are responsible for.
2. Which decisions about mental health services did you or your team make in the last year?

**The last real decision**

3. Think of the last time you had to decide where to put money or staff. Walk me through what happened, from the first conversation to the final decision.
4. What information did you look at? Where did it come from?
5. What did you wish you had known at the time?
6. Who else was involved, and what did they need to see?

**Data and tools today**

7. Which reports or dashboards do you open most often? What do you do with them?
8. Tell me about a time the data turned out to be wrong or misleading. How did you find out?
9. How do you compare your area with others? Which areas do you consider fair comparisons?

**Fairness and need**

10. How do you take deprivation or inequalities into account today? Can you give an example?

**Forward looking**

11. When did you last try to estimate future demand? How did you do it?
12. Have you ever modelled the effect of a change before making it, for example extra staff on a waiting list? What did you use?

**Close**

13. Is there anything I should have asked but did not?
14. Is there anyone else you think I should speak to?
