# How the MHSDS Monthly Statistics changed, July 2022 to August 2026

The Mental Health Services Monthly Statistics are the main open data source for MentalScape 2.0. Before building anything on them, every main data file from July 2022 to August 2026 was profiled, which is 50 monthly releases and about 24 million rows. Each release was then compared with the one before it.

This page explains what changed and what it means. The full month by month detail, generated by code, is in [mhsds_change_log_detail.md](mhsds_change_log_detail.md). The underlying tables are in [`data/reference`](../../data/reference). The code is in [`src/mentalscape/profiling`](../../src/mentalscape/profiling).

## The short version

1. **The publication grew a lot.** It went from 247 measures and 49 breakdowns in July 2022 to 399 measures and 110 breakdowns in August 2026. Files grew from about 320,000 rows to about 800,000.
2. **Change happens at the start of financial years.** Almost every measure change lands in April or July. Between those points the measure list is stable.
3. **429 different measure IDs appeared in total.** 217 were present in every release. 30 have been retired.
4. **A measure ID alone does not identify a number.** Some IDs are published for more than one reporting window in the same file, for example MHS32 as one month and as three months. The reporting window must be part of every key.
5. **Names are not reliable.** The same ID has carried different names over time, and sometimes two names in the same file. IDs and codes are the only safe keys.
6. **The same month can be published twice.** NHS England reissues some years as End of Year Final files, and those files carry later definitions, later measure lists and later area names back to earlier months.
7. **Twelve ICBs merged into six on 1 April 2026.** England went from 42 ICBs to 36. Sub ICB codes almost all survived, so they are the stable building block.
8. **Four in five values are suppressed,** and that has barely moved since 2022, from 77.9 to 80.5 percent.
9. **Some jumps are breaks and some are patterns.** Method changes cause one off breaks. School holidays and financial year resets cause jumps that repeat every year and must be modelled, not removed.
10. **The file format itself drifts.** Date styles, line endings and status labels all change, sometimes within one file.

## The five eras of the publication

| Era | Releases | Measures | Breakdowns | What defines it |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | July 2022 to March 2023 | 247 | 49 | First months on ICB and Sub ICB geography |
| 2 | April 2023 to March 2024 | 334 | 57 | 90 new measures, new crisis, liaison psychiatry and bed measures |
| 3 | April 2024 to June 2025 | 325 to 329 | 58 to 64 | MHSDS version 6, adult care cluster measures retired |
| 4 | July 2025 to March 2026 | 384 | 105 to 109 | Mental Health Act and inequality measures, file size doubles |
| 5 | April 2026 onwards | 399 | 106 to 110 | ICB mergers, new eating disorder measures, renamed headline measures |

Era 4 is described from the End of Year Final files, which NHS England reissued during 2026. The Performance files first published during 2025-26 were smaller (see theme 3 below).

## What changed, theme by theme

### 1. Measures added, removed and renamed

**April 2023** added 90 measures. 69 were general MHS measures, including bed and length of stay measures, 12 were crisis care (CCR) measures and 9 were a new liaison psychiatry (PLS) family. Three emergency crisis referral measures, CCR72, CCR72a and CCR72b, were retired. Nine crisis measures changed only in capitalisation, which is cosmetic but would break any code that matches on names.

**April 2024** was the first month of MHSDS version 6, which NHS England describes as a fundamental restructuring of how service type, ward details and delayed discharges are recorded. Nine measures were retired. They were the adult mental health care cluster measures (ACC33, ACC36, ACC37, MHS33), settled accommodation (ACC54, ACC62) and the older children's outcome measures (MHS92, MHS93, MHS94). Seven measures kept their ID but changed meaning or wording, for example MHS106 moved from "adult and older adult acute admissions" to "acute admissions", and MHS26 became "days of delayed discharge, for patients clinically ready for discharge".

**September 2024 and January 2025** each added a small number of measures. MHS148 counts open referrals with a presenting complaint, and NHS England later warned it is overstated by 25 to 30 percent. MHS156, MHS156a and MHS156b cover discharges, bed days and length of stay.

**July 2025** brought the largest single expansion. It added new Mental Health Act measures (MHS149 to MHS155, uses and lengths of detentions), rolling 12 month detention and community treatment order rates with ethnicity and deprivation gaps (MHS157 and MHS158), children and young people's access rates (MHS95a to MHS95h), community mental health outcomes (MHS160 to MHS162) and readmissions within 14 days (MHS163 to MHS165).

**April 2026** retired 18 early intervention in psychosis measures (EIP64 to EIP67 with their age splits, and EIP69a and EIP69b), added 33 new children's eating disorder measures including waiting time medians and 90th percentiles, and updated the eating disorder method. The age band in eating disorder names moved from "0 to 18" to "0 to 17". The headline measures for "people in contact with services" (MHS01, MH01, AMH01, CYP01, LDA01 and others) were renamed "people with an open referral". NHS England says the method is unchanged. The England and region breakdowns by Mental Health Act detention type and legal status disappeared in April and May 2026 and returned in June 2026, so any series built on them has a two month hole.

### 2. One ID, more than one number

The measure ID is not a unique key.

- **MHS32** appears twice in every release. "Referrals starting in RP" is the single month value, and "New referrals" is the rolling three month value. In August 2026 they were 446,269 and 1,492,487.
- From April 2026, **57 eating disorder measures** are published for both a rolling three month and a rolling 12 month window, with the same ID and the same name.
- The number of measures published on rolling windows grew sharply. Rolling 12 month measures went from 4 in 2022 to 86 in 2026.

So every value is identified by release version, reporting period start, reporting period end, breakdown, geography and measure ID together. A pipeline that pivots on measure ID and month alone would silently mix monthly and quarterly figures.

### 3. Performance and End of Year Final versions

Each month is first published as **Performance** data. For some years NHS England later reissues the whole year as **End of Year Final**, which includes late submissions and corrections. Release pages list final files for at least 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26.

Final files are not just corrected numbers. Because they are produced later, they also carry the later measure list, the later measure names and even the later area names. The July 2025 final file, for example, already uses "people with an open referral" and contains measures that were first published in December 2025. It also contains two names for 24 Sub ICB codes, the pre merger ICB name and the post merger one.

Mixing versions creates false changes. An early run of this analysis used the Performance file for August 2025 between Final files for July and September. Twenty one measures appeared to vanish in August and come back in September, 14 headline measures flipped between their old and new names, and MHS148 fell by 39 percent and then nearly doubled. All of it disappeared once the August Final file was used. The status column is also unreliable as a guide, with values of Final, Performance, `Performance ` with a trailing space, and Adhoc. Adhoc marks revised access measures in some 2024, 2025 and 2026 releases.

### 4. Geography

**ICB mergers, 1 April 2026.** Twelve ICBs were abolished and six new ones created, which are Essex, Central East, Thames Valley, Surrey and Sussex, Norfolk and Suffolk, and West and North London. England went from 42 ICBs to 36. Frimley was split up, so its Sub ICB code D4U1Y disappeared and a new Thames Valley Sub ICB, U2G6B, appeared. The other 23 affected Sub ICBs kept their codes and simply moved to a new parent ICB. NHS England notes that some Frimley postcodes were not remapped in time for April 2026, and that ONS population estimates for the new boundaries are not due until autumn 2026.

**Local government reorganisation, 1 April 2023.** Cumbria, North Yorkshire and Somerset county and district councils were replaced by four unitary authorities (Cumberland, Westmorland and Furness, North Yorkshire and Somerset). Twenty local authority codes disappeared and four appeared, taking the count from 333 to 317.

**Label drift.** The same Sub ICB geography appears as "Sub ICB - GP Practice or Residence", "Sub ICB of GP Practice or Residence" and, before April 2024, "Sub ICB of Residence". No measure appears under two labels in the same release, so the labels can safely be mapped to one level.

**Name conflicts.** The 2025-26 final files give 24 Sub ICB codes two names each, and label Frimley as "NHS FRIMLEY CCG", a body that was abolished in 2022. One local authority code flips between Tewkesbury and North Gloucestershire depending on the file version.

**The March 2026 final file uses post merger names.** It was produced after 1 April 2026, so it gives the 23 re-parented Sub ICBs only their new names, such as "NHS ESSEX ICB - 06Q", while its ICB rows still show the old 42 ICBs. A Sub ICB's name therefore points to an ICB that does not appear in that file. The warehouse keeps the ICB from the previous release for those rows and labels them as carried forward ([pipeline](pipeline.md)).

**A national breakdown with no geography in its label.** In 2022-23 the national split by ethnicity is labelled "Ethnicity (White British/Non-White British)", with no mention of England. It is mapped to England explicitly.

**Providers.** The number of providers reporting grew from 408 to 518, with a steady churn of providers joining and leaving.

### 5. File format

- **Dates** appear in five styles. Most files use `01/07/2022`. Others use `2023-06-01`, and the twelve 2023-24 files mix both in one file. Three more turned up when every End of Year Final file was loaded into the warehouse. The 2024-25 finals drop leading zeros (`1/4/2024`), the June 2024 final uses two digit years (`01/06/24`), and the April 2025 final uses hyphens (`01-04-2025`). Read carelessly, a two digit year becomes the year 24 and a reporting window appears to last 24,000 months.
- **Line endings** are usually Windows style, but most End of Year Final files mix Windows and Unix line endings in one file. Some CSV readers fail on this, as DuckDB did on August 2023 until the file was normalised.
- **File size** roughly doubled from July 2025, from about 400,000 rows to about 800,000, because of 45 new breakdowns by ethnicity, deprivation, age, learning disability and autism status, and Mental Health Act legal status.
- **Formatted Excel files** were dropped from April 2026. Only CSV is published.

### 6. Suppression

Between 77.9 and 80.5 percent of published values are suppressed in every release. Suppression is heaviest at provider level and lightest at England level. The share has not fallen as the publication grew, because every new breakdown creates many small cells. Usable detail is therefore concentrated at England, region, ICB and Sub ICB level.

### 7. Breaks versus patterns in the numbers

Sharp month on month jumps in England values were flagged automatically and labelled as one off or recurring.

**Recurring patterns that need to be modelled**

- **School holidays.** Referrals to children and young people's services (CYP32, MHS32a) fall by about 40 percent every August and recover in September. Closed referral outcome measures for children (MHS111 to MHS114) dip every July and August. December shows a smaller dip.
- **Financial year resets.** MHS69, a count of children and young people with at least two contacts, roughly triples every April and then settles, which suggests the count restarts at the start of each financial year.

**One off breaks that need to be handled**

- **MHS30f, attended community mental health contacts** fell by about two thirds in April 2024, when version 6 arrived, and roughly tripled in May 2025, when NHS England changed its method.
- **Crisis care referral measures** jumped in April 2023 when urgent and emergency care measures were redefined.
- **Version boundaries** cause artificial jumps. Between June 2025, a Performance file, and July 2025, an End of Year Final file, MHS148 rises by 83 percent and the very urgent crisis measures CCR118 and CCR119 fall by up to a half. These jumps sit exactly on the switch between versions, so they say more about the files than about services.

### 8. Data quality notices from NHS England

These notices, taken from the release pages, must travel with the affected measures.

| Measure | Issue |
|---|---|
| MHS148 | Estimated 25 to 30 percent higher than the true value, not corrected for 2024-25 |
| EIP68 | Some referrals counted more than once, an overcount of about 23 percent in March 2025, corrected from July 2026 onwards |
| IMD based rates | Rates for MHS95, MHS116, MHS157, MHS158 and out of area placements were too low in some 2025 releases, removed and corrected in the final data |
| Frimley | Postcode mapping for April 2026 in month measures not updated in time |
| Restraints | End of year file reissued in August 2026 after an error |

## What this means for MentalScape 2.0

1. **Keys.** Every value is keyed on release version, reporting window, breakdown, geography code and measure ID. Never on names.
2. **Versions.** The raw layer keeps every version. The staging layer prefers End of Year Final where it exists and records which version each value came from ([ADR 0003](../adr/0003-time-window-and-release-versions.md)).
3. **Geography.** Sub ICB codes are the building block, with a dated lookup to old and new ICBs ([ADR 0004](../adr/0004-geography-harmonisation.md)).
4. **Measure catalogue.** [`mhsds_measure_catalogue.csv`](../../data/reference/mhsds_measure_catalogue.csv) records every ID's first and last appearance and its name history. Models only use measures with a continuous history, or handle the break explicitly.
5. **Data tests.** The warehouse fails on missing Sub ICBs, new date styles, unknown status labels and windows, duplicate values and gaps in the months. It reads mixed line endings from a normalised copy. It warns, rather than fails, on measure IDs the change log has never seen, on Sub ICBs with no ICB and on large jumps in England totals, because those are often real and need a person to judge them. The full list is in the [pipeline guide](pipeline.md).
6. **Seasonality.** Forecasts include school term and financial year effects.
7. **Caveats.** The data quality notices above are stored as data and shown wherever the measure appears.

## How this was produced

```
make profile     # profile every main data file
make changelog   # compare releases and write the reference tables and detail report
```

The profiler reads each file with DuckDB and writes summary tables. The change log compares each release with the one before. Both are tested in CI.

**Limits of this analysis.** It covers the main data file only, not the separate out of area placements, referral spells or restrictive interventions files. It compares the versions held at the time, which are End of Year Final for 2023-24 and 2025-26, Performance for 2024-25 and 2026-27, and files labelled Final in the status column for 2022-23. In Phase 1 the automated pipeline will collect every version of every file and this page will be regenerated.
